\begin{abstract}
Conditional flow matching is normally trained for one target distribution at a time. We
consider instead a velocity field indexed by a \emph{set} of interventions, so that the
distributional response to a combination never observed during training is a prediction
rather than a fit. The obstacle is geometric: models in this setting act in a learned
latent space that is defined only up to a diffeomorphism, and the operation they almost
universally perform --- adding one displacement per intervention --- is not invariant under
that reparameterisation. We give IHC-FM, in which the velocity field is assembled as the
metric-inverse of a pulled-back ambient one-form, $v = G^{-1}J^{\top}b$ with
$G = J^{\top}W J$. Because every ingredient is a function of the \emph{observed} point,
each carries the correct tensor valence and the whole learned field transforms as a vector
field, for arbitrary learned decoder, metric and one-form: measured relative residual
$1.63\times10^{-15}$ analytically and $7.26\times10^{-16}$ for the implemented field. On top
of this we place a permutation-invariant low-rank interaction hierarchy whose interaction
term is \emph{bit-exactly} zero on singletons ($0.0$, $15/15$ cases) at $O(kr)$ rather than
$O(k^{2})$ cost ($121\times$ speedup at $k{=}32$), a boundary-correct exposure response, and
a saturation operator expressed in the chart-invariant metric norm --- whose coordinate
alternatives we measure to break covariance at $2.2\times10^{-2}$. We also report an
evaluation-validity result that we believe matters beyond this method: the CFM loss is
\textbf{not comparable across couplings}, because it tracks the identity-pair fraction a
coupling produces, and on our data the coupling ranking inverts between CFM loss and a
distributional metric. Two case studies validate the construction and are reported with
their negatives: IHC-FM beats an additive-displacement baseline on both
(organoid screen $-1.577$ $[-2.100, -1.101]$ over $136$ paired populations; three of four
purged financial splits with CIs excluding zero), while \emph{losing} to the no-change
control on the organoid screen ($+0.688$ $[+0.351, +1.058]$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================================

A flow-matching model is usually asked to transport one source distribution to one target.
A different question arises whenever a system can be acted on in several ways at once: given
a finite set of primitive interventions, each applied at some scalar exposure, predict the
distributional response to a \emph{subset that was never observed}. Writing $\Pset$ for the
active subset and $\tau$ for its exposures, we want a single velocity field
$v_{\theta}(t, z \mid \Pset, \tau)$ whose time-one flow pushes the pre-intervention
population onto the post-intervention one, for subsets outside the training support.

The dominant modelling pattern encodes each intervention $p$ as a latent displacement
$\delta_{p}$ and predicts a combination by summing them. Our starting observation is that
this is not a coordinate-free operation. The latent space in which the sum is taken is
learned, and nothing in a reconstruction or flow-matching objective distinguishes $z$ from
$\psi(z)$ for a diffeomorphism $\psi$. A vector \emph{field} transforms linearly under
$\psi$ with no remainder; a finite \emph{displacement} does not, and picks up a defect that
is second order in its norm (Proposition~\ref{prop:defect}). A modelling operation intended
to express a fact about interventions therefore depends on an arbitrary choice of chart.

Our response is not to correct the defect term by term but to build a field for which the
question does not arise. IHC-FM parameterises the velocity as
\begin{equation}
v(t, z \mid \Pset, \tau) \;=\; G(z)^{-1} J(z)^{\top} b\bigl(t, \Dec(z), c, a(\tau, \Pset)\bigr),
\qquad G = J^{\top} W(\Dec(z))\, J ,
\label{eq:field}
\end{equation}
where $\Dec$ is a decoder to an observed space, $J$ its Jacobian, $W$ an ambient
positive-definite weight, and $b$ an ambient one-form built as a hierarchy over the
intervention set. Every ingredient is a function of the observed point $\Dec(z)$, which is
invariant under a change of latent chart; consequently $G$ is a $(0,2)$ tensor, $J^{\top}b$
is a $(0,1)$ tensor, and $v$ is a vector field --- \emph{by valence, for arbitrary learned
components}, with no term-by-term repair (Proposition~\ref{prop:cov}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A chart-covariant compositional velocity field} (\S\ref{sec:method},
      \S\ref{sec:pullback}). The pullback construction of
      Eq.~\ref{eq:field} makes the entire learned field transform as a vector field
      (measured $1.63\times10^{-15}$ analytically, $7.26\times10^{-16}$ for the implemented
      field, stable to $9.61\times10^{-16}$ under a $1000\times$ drive stress). Covariance
      follows from the \emph{valences} of the objects, so it does not depend on any learned
      quantity being correct --- and it forbids the routine Euclidean stabiliser
      $G \to G + \epsilon I$, whose cost we measure (\S\ref{sec:props}).
\item \textbf{A structurally-identified interaction hierarchy.} Interactions enter through a
      second set moment, giving permutation invariance to $7.8\times10^{-16}$ and
      \emph{bit-exact} vanishing on singletons and on zero exposures, at $O(kr)$ cost
      (\S\ref{sec:hier}), driven by a boundary-correct exposure response
      (Eq.~\ref{eq:hill}, \S\ref{sec:dose}) and bounded by a chart-invariant saturation
      operator (\S\ref{sec:sat}). Because the interaction term is exactly zero on every singleton,
      leave-one-combination-out is a genuine test of it rather than a re-fit.
\item \textbf{An evaluation-validity result about couplings.} The CFM loss tracks the
      identity-pair fraction a coupling induces and is therefore \emph{not} comparable
      across couplings; we measure a ranking reversal in which the coupling that is worst on
      CFM loss by a factor of eight is \emph{best} on a distributional metric, while the
      exact-assignment coupling sits at the good end of the CFM-loss column and is worse
      than predicting no change distributionally (\S\ref{sec:cfm}).
\item \textbf{Two case studies reported with their negatives}, against a
      parameter-matched baseline ladder whose statistical power we measure and report
      (\S\ref{sec:baselines}; Table~\ref{\tabonelabel} and Figure~\ref{fig:table1} are
      generated externally and are placeholders in this build), including a
      headline negative --- IHC-FM loses to the no-change control on one of them
      (\S\ref{sec:exp}, \S\ref{sec:limits}). Positioning against prior flow-matching,
      perturbation-modelling and benchmark-validity work is in \S\ref{sec:related}, and
      hyperparameters in Appendix~\ref{app:hparams}.
\end{enumerate}

A retraction belongs in the contributions list rather than a footnote. An earlier version of
this work centred a connection-corrected pairwise coupling term added to the instantaneous
velocity. Our own diagnosis shows the justification was circular: integrating the summed
field \emph{already} produces the symmetric kinematic cross term, so the added term inserts a
second, collinear copy of it (\S\ref{sec:doublecount}). That term survives here only as an
ablation arm.

\section{The compositional velocity field}
\label{sec:method}
% =====================================================================================

Throughout, a state is a point $z$ in a latent chart $Z = \Rd$; an \emph{intervention} is an
index $p$ into a finite set $\{1, \dots, k\}$; an \emph{exposure} is a scalar
$\tau_{p} \ge 0$; a \emph{population} is an empirical measure on an observed space
$\RD$. Nothing else is assumed about what these indices mean.

\subsection{Chart covariance by valence}
\label{sec:pullback}

Let $\Dec : Z \to \RD$ be a $C^{1}$ immersion with Jacobian $J_{\Dec}(z)$ of full column
rank, let $W(x)$ be a positive-definite weight on the observed space, and let $b(x)$ be an
observed-space one-form. Define
\begin{equation}
G(z) = J_{\Dec}^{\top} W(\Dec(z)) J_{\Dec}, \qquad
\alpha(z) = J_{\Dec}^{\top} b(\Dec(z)), \qquad
v(z) = G(z)^{-1}\alpha(z).
\label{eq:pullback}
\end{equation}
A change of latent chart is a diffeomorphism $\psi$ acting on $z$ alone, with the decoder
carried along as $\Dec' = \Dec \circ \psi^{-1}$. The observed point is then an
\emph{invariant} of the re-chart, $\Dec'(\psi(z)) = \Dec(z)$, and every consequence follows
mechanically: $J_{\Dec'} = J_{\Dec} J^{-1}$ with $J = D\psi$, hence
$G' = J^{-\top} G J^{-1}$ and $\alpha' = J^{-\top}\alpha$, hence $v' = J v$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:cov}). The design rule this imposes is total and easy to violate:
\emph{any} scalar, vector or tensor the field depends on must be a function of the observed
point $x$ (or of $t, \tau, c$, which are chart-blind), never of the latent coordinate $z$.

The rule has teeth. It forbids the additive Euclidean stabiliser $G \to G + \epsilon I$,
because $I$ is not a $(0,2)$ tensor; conditioning must instead be applied in the observed
space, $W \to W + w_{\min} I_{D}$, which is covariant precisely because $x$ is not being
transformed. We measure both halves of this contrast in \S\ref{sec:props}.

\subsection{Exposure response with correct boundaries}
\label{sec:dose}

Exposure enters through a per-intervention amplitude $a_{p}(\tau_{p}) \ge 0$, which we take
from the Hill family written in its numerically correct form,
\begin{equation}
a_{p}(\tau) = A_{p}\,\sigma\bigl(h_{p}(\log \tau - \log e_{p})\bigr) \quad (\tau > 0),
\qquad a_{p}(0) = 0 ,
\label{eq:hill}
\end{equation}
with $A_{p} = e^{\ell_{p}}$, $h_{p} > 0$, $e_{p} > 0$ learned per intervention and $\sigma$
the logistic function. This is algebraically the textbook
$A\tau^{h}/(\tau^{h} + e^{h})$, but evaluating it as a logistic of a log-ratio has no
$\tau^{h}$ and no division, so there is no overflow or $0/0$ at either endpoint. Three
properties are then structural rather than fitted: $a_{p}(0) = 0$ \emph{bit-exactly} (via a
branch on $\tau > 0$, with the standard double-\texttt{where} so the backward pass stays
finite when $h < 1$); $a_{p}$ is strictly increasing on $\tau > 0$ because $\sigma$ and
$\log$ are; and $a_{p} < A_{p}$ strictly, so boundedness is a ceiling rather than a clamp.
The exact zero is what the interaction hierarchy's vanishing property rests on.

\subsection{A permutation-invariant low-rank interaction hierarchy}
\label{sec:hier}

Let $e_{p} \in \mathbb{R}^{r}$ be a learned intervention embedding and $u_{p} = a_{p}e_{p}$.
Define two set moments and the one-form
\begin{align}
m_{1} &= \sum_{p} u_{p}, &
m_{2} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl( m_{1} \odot m_{1} - \sum_{p} u_{p} \odot u_{p} \Bigr), \\
b(t, x, c) &= A_{1}(t, x, c, m_{1})\, m_{1} \;+\; C_{2}(t, x, c, m_{1})\, m_{2},
\label{eq:hierarchy}
\end{align}
where $A_{1}, C_{2} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times r}$ are produced by two heads and $c$ is an
optional population code. Both branches are \emph{structurally multiplied by their moment},
and three properties follow from that form rather than from training.

\textbf{(H1) Permutation invariance.} $m_{1}$ and $m_{2}$ are symmetric functions of the
multiset $\{(a_{p}, e_{p})\}$, so $b$ is invariant to any consistent relabelling of the
intervention indices.

\textbf{(H2) Bit-exact singleton vanishing.} $m_{2}$ collects exactly the cross terms
$a_{p}a_{q}$, $p \neq q$, so if at most one amplitude is nonzero then $m_{2} = 0.0$
\emph{exactly} and the interaction contribution is the exact zero vector. The grouping in
Eq.~\ref{eq:hierarchy} is load-bearing: forming $u_{p}$ \emph{first} makes the singleton case
a bit-identical cancellation $u_{p}\odot u_{p} - u_{p}\odot u_{p} = 0.0$, whereas the
algebraically equal $\tfrac12[(\sum a_{p}e_{p})^{2} - \sum a_{p}^{2}e_{p}^{2}]$ rounds the two
terms differently and leaves an $O(10^{-17})$ residual. The same argument covers a zero
exposure inside a larger set.

\textbf{(H3) Zero-exposure identity.} The main branch is likewise multiplied by $m_{1}$, so
$a = 0$ gives $b \equiv 0$ exactly and the flow is the identity map. A model with an
unmultiplied main head would have to \emph{learn} that a null intervention does nothing.

\textbf{Cost.} Both moments are $O(kr)$; no $k^{2}$ object is ever formed. The explicit
double loop is retained only as a numerical oracle, and the two agree to round-off
($1.4\times10^{-15}$), so the cost claim concerns the same quantity computed more cheaply
rather than an approximation.

\subsection{Saturation in the invariant norm}
\label{sec:sat}

Bounding the field's magnitude is a practical necessity and a covariance trap: any bound
expressed through a coordinate-dependent quantity silently converts the field into a
non-vector object. We bound the \emph{metric} norm, which is invariant
($v'^{\top}G'v' = v^{\top}Gv$), by a ceiling read off the observed point:
\begin{equation}
n(z) = \sqrt{v^{\top} G v}, \qquad
\kappa(x) = \mathrm{softplus}(\mathrm{head}(x)) + \kappa_{\min} > 0, \qquad
v_{\mathrm{sat}} = v \cdot \frac{\kappa}{n}\tanh\!\Bigl(\frac{n}{\kappa}\Bigr).
\label{eq:sat}
\end{equation}
The bracket is a chart-invariant scalar, so $v_{\mathrm{sat}}$ is exactly parallel to $v$ and
transforms exactly as $v$ does; $\|v_{\mathrm{sat}}\|_{G} = \kappa\tanh(n/\kappa) < \kappa$
in exact arithmetic, with $v_{\mathrm{sat}} \to v$ as $n \to 0$, so the ceiling is an
asymptote and the field is untouched where it is small. The removable singularity at $n=0$
is handled by the second-order expansion $1 - u^{2}/3$.

Two ablation arms exist because the contrast is the justification for the component: a
coordinate scalar gate $v\,\sigma(\mathrm{net}([z,v]))$, and the same radial form built from
the Euclidean norm $\|v\|_{2}$ and $\kappa(z)$. Both are non-covariant by construction, and
\S\ref{sec:props} reports how badly.

\section{The flow-matching objective, and why its loss is not a ranking key}
\label{sec:cfm}
% =====================================================================================

\paragraph{Path and target.} We use the linear (I-CFM) interpolant in the observed space
\citep{lipman2022fm,tong2023otcfm}. For a coupling $\pi_{c}$ of the pre- and
post-intervention populations under condition $c$, draw $(x_{0}, x_{1}) \sim \pi_{c}$ and
$t \sim U(0,1)$, and set
\begin{equation}
x_{t} = (1-t)x_{0} + t x_{1} + \sigma\varepsilon, \qquad
u(t \mid x_{0}, x_{1}) = x_{1} - x_{0}, \qquad
\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}\,\bigl\| v_{\theta}(t, x_{t} \mid \Pset, \tau) - u \bigr\|^{2}.
\label{eq:cfm}
\end{equation}
The conditional velocity is $t$-independent for this path ($\sigma > 0$ leaves it unchanged),
but the \emph{marginal} field $v(t,x) = \mathbb{E}[x_{1}-x_{0} \mid x_{t}=x]$ is genuinely
$t$-dependent, which is why the field takes $t$ as an input. Our implementation matches the
analytic conditional velocity to $6.7\times10^{-16}$.

\paragraph{The coupling is computed in the observed space}, never from latent coordinates: a
coupling built in a learned chart is a function of the current encoder and drifts during
training, so it is not a property of the data. Four options are selectable --- product,
exact minibatch assignment, entropic (Sinkhorn, optionally unbalanced), and index-aligned
\citep{tong2023otcfm}. We select \texttt{sinkhorn} with $\varepsilon = 0.05$, $25$
iterations, unbalanced relaxation $\tau_{\mathrm{ub}} = 1.0$ on a held-out distributional
score.

\paragraph{The CFM loss is not comparable across couplings.} This is a methodological point
rather than an implementation note, and it is measured rather than argued. The loss in
Eq.~\ref{eq:cfm} rewards a coupling that pairs points with themselves, because a
self-paired sample has a target of zero variance. The identity-pair fraction our couplings
produce spans a factor of $256$ --- index-aligned $1.0$, exact assignment $0.795$, entropic
$0.229$, product $0.0039$ --- so the achievable loss floor is a property
of the coupling and not of the model. Selecting on it is therefore invalid. The consequence
is not hypothetical: ranking three couplings by held-out CFM loss and by normalised energy
distance \emph{reverses} the ordering at both ends. The product coupling is worst on CFM
loss by a factor of eight ($6.9873$ against $0.86$ for the other two) and yet \emph{best} on
the distributional metric ($0.8094$); the exact-assignment coupling is at the good end of the
CFM-loss column ($0.8600$, within $0.3\%$ of the selected coupling's $0.8579$ --- and we
attach no significance to that ordering, for the reason this paragraph is about)
and yet scores $1.1188$ on the distributional metric --- \textbf{worse than
predicting no change} (Table~\ref{tab:coupling}, Appendix~\ref{app:policy}). We accordingly
fix a distributional metric as the only ranking key in code, and report CFM loss as a
within-coupling training diagnostic. A minibatch OT coupling is also not the paired coupling
even when the target is a deterministic function of the source: on an affine test system
exact assignment agrees with the true pairing on only $79.5\%$ of samples. A model fitted
under one coupling must not be scored against the closed-form marginal of another.

\paragraph{Training schedule.} A jointly-trained interaction branch absorbs single-intervention
effects, after which its ``interaction'' is a relabelled main effect. We therefore train in
three stages: (1) main effects on singleton conditions only, interaction branch \emph{disabled}
rather than penalised; (2) interaction branch enabled on combinations, with the stage-1 model
frozen as an anchor penalising drift of the singleton velocities; (3) joint fine-tune with
singletons oversampled and an interaction-norm penalty whose weight is selected on held-out
combinations. Non-finite steps are skipped and counted rather than allowed to poison
parameters; every run reported here had $0$.

\section{Three propositions}
\label{sec:props}
% =====================================================================================

The statements below are exactly those verified in our propositions document; we have taken
care that each claims no more than its proof gives, and each carries its scope limit.
Derivatives in the verification are computed by complex-step differentiation
($h = 10^{-20}$), which has no truncation error and no subtractive cancellation, so residuals
are limited by float64 round-off ($\sim\!10^{-16}$) rather than by a finite-difference floor.
Each headline residual is the value at the reported reference configuration. For
Propositions~\ref{prop:conn} and~\ref{prop:cov} the maximum over $9$ independent seeds is
given alongside it ($1.06\times$ and $2.40\times$ the reference value respectively);
Proposition~\ref{prop:defect} is verified as a convergence \emph{rate} rather than a single
residual, so no seed maximum is quoted for it. Full proofs are in
Appendix~\ref{app:props}, the ablation arms that act as failing controls in
Appendix~\ref{app:ablations} (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}), and the measured residuals are
summarised in Figure~\ref{fig:theory}.

\begin{proposition}[Displacement defect]
\label{prop:defect}
Let $\psi$ be $C^{2}$ near $z$. Then
$\psi(z+\delta) - \psi(z) = D\psi(z)\delta + R(z,\delta)$ with
$R = \tfrac12 D^{2}\psi(z)[\delta,\delta] + O(\|\delta\|^{3})$. Hence: composing vector
\emph{fields} and integrating is chart-covariant, because a field transforms linearly with no
remainder, whereas adding finite \emph{displacements} incurs $R$; if $\psi$ is affine then
$D^{2}\psi \equiv 0$ and $R \equiv 0$ \emph{exactly}; and the relative defect
$\|R\|/\|\delta\|$ is proportional to $\|\delta\|$ to leading order.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\textbf{Verification.} Log-log slope of the defect norm against $\|\delta\|$ is
$1.999142$ (deviation $8.58\times10^{-4}$ from $2$) on $\|\delta\| \le 10^{-2}$; the affine
control gives $2.46\times10^{-16}$, machine zero; the relative defect grows like $\tau^{1}$
(slope $0.99215$).
\textbf{Scope.} This is a statement about \emph{displacements}, not about model accuracy. It
does not assert that displacement-additive models fail, nor that the defect dominates their
error. Two directly relevant negatives: at fixed $\|\delta\|$, latent anisotropy does
\emph{not} increase the defect (relative range $8.05\times10^{-3}$ across a $20\times$
stretch, no trend), so the only supportable anisotropy claim is the indirect one via
$\|\delta\|$-inflation; and the sub-$10^{-4}$ rise in measured relative error is
cancellation in the \emph{measurement} (absolute residual $1.04\times10^{-16}$ against a
predicted round-off floor of $1.51\times10^{-16}$), not a missing term.

\begin{proposition}[Connection law and the tensoriality of $S + 2\Gamma$]
\label{prop:conn}
Let $X, Y$ be vector fields, $G$ a $C^{2}$ Riemannian metric with Levi-Civita connection
$\Gamma$, and $\psi$ a $C^{3}$ diffeomorphism with $J = D\psi$. With
$S = DX[Y] + DY[X]$ and all tilded objects read at $\psi(z)$:
$\tilde S = J S + 2 D^{2}\psi[X,Y]$; $\tilde\Gamma[JX, JY] = J\Gamma[X,Y] - D^{2}\psi[X,Y]$;
and therefore $\tilde S + 2\tilde\Gamma = J(S + 2\Gamma)$. The coefficient is forced: for
$S + c\Gamma$ the defect is $(2-c)D^{2}\psi$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\textbf{Verification.} $S + 2\Gamma$ is tensorial to $2.971\times10^{-15}$
($\le 3.160\times10^{-15}$ over $9$ seeds), the connection law holds to
$4.585\times10^{-15}$, and the cross-term defect matches $2D^{2}\psi[X,Y]$ to
$2.594\times10^{-14}$. Controls fail as they must: $S - 2\Gamma$ at $5.335\times10^{-2}$
(and at every one of $9$ seeds, minimum $4.954\times10^{-2}$), $S$ alone at
$3.996\times10^{-2}$, and an unsymmetrised $D^{2}\psi$ at $8.341\times10^{-2}$.
\textbf{Scope, and a warning.} Under an \emph{affine} chart all of $S+2\Gamma$, $S-2\Gamma$
and $S$ transform correctly ($3.102\times10^{-15}$), so an affine map cannot identify the
coefficient. PCA and any whitening are affine; a covariance experiment run on PCA-whitened
coordinates would report success for a model with the wrong sign. Note also that this
proposition repairs \emph{one hand-built term}, which is a strictly weaker guarantee than
Proposition~\ref{prop:cov}, and that \S\ref{sec:doublecount} explains why the repaired term
is nonetheless not what IHC-FM uses.

\begin{proposition}[Whole-field covariance of the pullback construction]
\label{prop:cov}
Let $\Dec$ be a $C^{1}$ immersion, $W$ positive definite and $C^{0}$ as a function of the
\emph{observed} point, $b$ an observed-space one-form, and define $G, \alpha, v$ as in
Eq.~\ref{eq:pullback}. Let $\psi$ be a diffeomorphism with $J = D\psi(z)$ and read the same
decoder in the new chart, $\Dec' = \Dec\circ\psi^{-1}$. Then
$G' = J^{-\top} G J^{-1}$, $\alpha' = J^{-\top}\alpha$, and $v' = J v$: $v$ is a vector
field, for arbitrary $W$, arbitrary $b$, arbitrary immersion $\Dec$ and arbitrary $\psi$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\textbf{Verification.} With a \emph{random nonlinear} decoder and a \emph{random
nonlinear} chart, whole-field covariance holds to $\mathbf{1.629\times10^{-15}}$ (mean
$8.83\times10^{-16}$; $\le 3.904\times10^{-15}$ over $9$ seeds), with $G$ a $(0,2)$ tensor to
$9.19\times10^{-16}$ and $\alpha$ a $(0,1)$ tensor to $7.43\times10^{-16}$. The chart-2
decoder Jacobian is obtained by complex-step differentiating the \emph{actually composed} map
$z' \mapsto \Dec(\psi^{-1}(z'))$, rather than by substituting the chain rule, which would make
the test a tautology; the chain rule is separately checked at $4.59\times10^{-16}$. For the
\emph{implemented} field the residual is $7.26\times10^{-16}$, rising only to
$8.64\times10^{-16}$ at $50\times$ drive and $9.61\times10^{-16}$ at $1000\times$; the
pullback metric is SPD with minimum eigenvalue $0.514$, condition number $\le 5.72$, no
solver fallbacks, and an analytic Jacobian agreeing with autodiff to $2.41\times10^{-16}$.
\textbf{Scope.} Four assumptions are load-bearing and one is a hard constraint on the
method. $\Dec$ must be an immersion on the data region (measured
$\sigma_{\min}(J_{\Dec}) = 0.461$), $W$ must be positive definite \emph{and a function of the
observed point}, $G$ must be well conditioned for the numerical residual to be small
(covariance is exact in exact arithmetic for any invertible $G$), and $\psi$ must be a
diffeomorphism on the region. Finally, \textbf{no Euclidean $\epsilon I$ may be added to the
latent metric}: the error is linear in $\epsilon$, reaching $2.089\times10^{-2}$ analytically
and $2.60\times10^{-3}$ on the implemented field at the routine value $\epsilon = 10^{-2}$,
\ie thirteen orders of magnitude worse than the unstabilised construction. Conditioning must
be applied in the observed space, where it is covariant ($7.79\times10^{-16}$ with an ambient
floor). One further limit deserves emphasis: this is an \emph{operator-level} guarantee about
a single chart. It says the field transforms correctly; it does not say a model trained in
chart~1 and a model trained in chart~2 will learn the same field, and \S\ref{sec:limits}
reports that they measurably do not.

\paragraph{The saturation contrast.} Proposition~\ref{prop:cov} also explains why
Eq.~\ref{eq:sat} is written in the metric norm. Under the same test, the metric-radial form
preserves covariance ($7.26\times10^{-16}$) and preserves direction to
$2.22\times10^{-16}$, while the coordinate-scalar gate breaks covariance at
$2.20\times10^{-2}$ and the Euclidean-norm variant at $6.20\times10^{-3}$ --- a contrast of
roughly thirteen orders of magnitude, which is the entire justification for the component.
Under a $1000\times$ drive stress the attained norm equals $\kappa$ to
$+4.44\times10^{-16}$, so we state the guarantee as
$\|v_{\mathrm{sat}}\|_{G} \le \kappa(1 + 10^{-12})$ rather than as a strict inequality.

\subsection{Why the connection coupling was replaced}
\label{sec:doublecount}

For fields $X, Y$ and integration time $T$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{flow}_{X+Y}^{T}(z) - \bigl[z + (\mathrm{flow}_{X}^{T}(z) - z) + (\mathrm{flow}_{Y}^{T}(z) - z)\bigr]
= \tfrac{T^{2}}{2}\,\bigl(DX[Y] + DY[X]\bigr) + O(T^{3}).
\label{eq:free}
\end{equation}
Integrating the summed field \emph{already} produces the symmetric kinematic cross term, for
free. The previous justification for an explicit $\beta S$ term --- that $S$ is the leading
term of joint-minus-additive behaviour --- therefore proves the opposite of its conclusion:
$S$ is what generator composition yields, so adding it to the velocity inserts a second copy.
The two copies enter at different orders in $T$ (composition's in the displacement at
$O(T^{2})$, the hand-added one at $O(T)$), so their ratio $2\beta/T$ \emph{diverges} as
$T \to 0$: measured $40\times$ at $T = 0.05$ and $400\times$ at $T = 0.005$. They are also
collinear (minimum cosine $0.99999790$), so the added term supplies no new direction.
Eq.~\ref{eq:free} is verified at relative error $1.610\times10^{-3}$ at $T = 0.005$ with
log-log convergence slope $0.99971$ against the predicted $O(T)$.

What can be added is therefore only what lies \emph{outside} the kinematic span, which is
exactly the specification of \S\ref{sec:hier}: non-kinematic, singleton-vanishing, low-rank.
The connection-coupling form is retained as an ablation arm, since it is the correct control
for the claim that the non-kinematic branch does work the kinematic term cannot.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
% =====================================================================================

\paragraph{Flow matching.} We use the conditional flow-matching objective
\citep{lipman2022fm} with a linear interpolant \citep{liu2022rectified} and the coupling
generalisation of I-CFM/OT-CFM \citep{tong2023otcfm}, and claim no improvement to the
estimator itself; our addition is what is \emph{parameterised}, plus the coupling
non-comparability result of \S\ref{sec:cfm}, which that line states as a variance-reduction
benefit but not as a constraint on model selection. Meta Flow Matching
\citep{atanackovic2024mfm} amortises flow matching over populations by conditioning the
velocity on an embedding of the initial measure. We take that mechanism directly and claim
no credit for it --- our population encoder \emph{is} that idea --- and we report that we
measured it to be harmful on exactly the combinations this method targets, so it is off by
default (\S\ref{sec:exp}). What MFM does not do is compose several interventions: it
conditions one field on a population, whereas ours is indexed by an intervention set.
Metric Flow Matching \citep{kapusniak2024metricfm} and Energy-Guided Geometric Flow Matching
\citep{zweig2025egfm} both use a learned geometry, but to curve the interpolating
\emph{path} so it follows the data manifold. We leave the path straight and put the geometry
in the \emph{field}: a metric used to bend a path does not make the field a vector field.

\paragraph{Perturbation modelling.} The compositional perturbation autoencoder
\citep{lotfollahi2023cpa} encodes each perturbation as a latent displacement and predicts a
combination by summing them with a per-perturbation dose scaling; GEARS-class models
\citep{roohani2023gears} compose graph-informed per-perturbation representations under the
same additive rule. The relevant class property is the composition rule, not the priors, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:defect} is a statement about that rule. Our competitor is a
\emph{correct} implementation of the class --- bit-exactly additive, zero-exposure-vanishing
and permutation-invariant, parameter-matched to $0.993\times$ under an identical protocol
--- rather than a weakened one.

\paragraph{Benchmark validity.} \citet{ahlmanneltze2025linear} report that deep
perturbation-effect predictors do not beat simple baselines including no-change, and
\citet{miller2025rebuttal} respond that the conclusion turns on metric calibration. We do
not adjudicate. We adopt the shared methodological lesson --- that the choice of metric can
reverse a ranking --- as a protocol requirement: every normalised figure here carries its
no-change denominator, the no-change control is a first-class row, and we report the
concrete reversal we measured in our own coupling and baseline comparisons
(\S\ref{sec:cfm}). That is also the source of our headline negative
(\S\ref{sec:limits}).

\paragraph{Learned intervention fields with Lie-geometric structure.} Learned intervention
response fields with bracket geometry are the contribution of recent causal-discovery work
\citep{mahadevan2026bridge}; \textbf{we do not introduce learned Lie-geometric intervention
fields and do not claim to}. Three separations. That work recovers graph structure under
latent confounding, whereas we predict a distributional response and never estimate a graph.
Its stated scope is single-node intervention targets, whereas our object is the $k \ge 2$
composition. And bracket geometry uses the antisymmetric part $[X,Y]$, which is already a
tensor and never meets the transformation defect; the object carrying the \emph{magnitude} of
a composition is the symmetric part $S$, which is not (Proposition~\ref{prop:conn}).

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}
% =====================================================================================

We score with normalised energy distance,
$\ED(\hat\mu, \mu)/\ED(\mu_{0}, \mu)$, where $\mu_{0}$ is the pre-intervention population, so
$1.0$ means no better than predicting no change and $0$ is perfect. Every normalised figure
is reported with its denominator, and every CI is a grouped bootstrap over populations or
blocks --- never over individual samples or overlapping windows. Metrics are validated
against known answers (identical inputs $0.0$; a no-change prediction normalising to exactly
$1.0$; unbiased ED mean $0.0012 \pm 0.0037$ under the null while the V-statistic is biased
upward at $0.0575$).

\paragraph{A single fold cannot order these models, and we report that instead of a ranking.}
Across seeds $0/1/2$ on one fold the neural rows swing wildly while the controls are

\subsection{The interaction hierarchy's value is budget-dependent, and invisible at the
budget the baselines were matched at}
\label{sec:budget}

Under the shared $800$-step protocol of Table~\ref{\tabonelabel}, the within-model ablation
is a null: disabling the interaction pathway changes the paired score by $+0.015$
$[-0.024, +0.051]$, and the full model is nominally $0.015$ nED \emph{worse}. We report
that, and we also report why it is the wrong number to conclude from.

An oracle decomposition first rules out a floor effect. Integrating the true field on the
same pre-cells a model sees gives an achievable floor of $0.018$ nED; deleting the true
interaction term from that same oracle costs $0.292$ nED. So the benchmark does contain
substantial interaction signal --- roughly $0.41$ of the additive velocity magnitude --- and
a null cannot be blamed on there being nothing to find.

Holding the architecture, the splits, the coupling and $r$ fixed and varying \emph{only} the
step budget (4 folds $\times$ 2 seeds, Table~\ref{tab:budget}) resolves it. The paired effect
is monotone in the budget and becomes decisive at $6400$ steps: $-0.175$ $[-0.275, -0.076]$,
CI excluding zero, favouring the full model on \textbf{all $8$} fold--seed cells (sign test
$p=0.0078$).

The interaction branch therefore does not wait for the main effects to saturate --- it simply
needs more optimisation steps than they do, and at $800$ steps it has not trained enough to
contribute at all. \textbf{Under a shared step budget, an interaction branch is silently
undertrained relative to the main-effect branch it sits on top of.} For anyone
parameter-matching a compositional model against flat baselines, this is the practical
warning: matching parameters and steps does not match convergence, and a compositional
component can be scored as worthless by a protocol that is fair in every respect except
the one that matters to it.

the fair one against baselines matched at that budget and it stays in
Table~\ref{\tabonelabel}; the $6400$-step result establishes that the mechanism works when

protocol axis. Scope: $n=8$ cells rather than Table~\ref{\tabonelabel}'s $21$ folds, one
benchmark instance, $r$ fixed at $2$ rather than re-tuned per budget, and no budget reaches
the $0.018$ floor. Re-running the full nine-arm table at $6400$ steps costs roughly eight

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
% =====================================================================================

Stated plainly, because each of these is measured.

\textbf{IHC-FM loses to the no-change control on the organoid screen.} The paired difference
is $+0.688$ $[+0.351, +1.058]$ over $136$ populations. It beats the additive baseline
decisively on the same populations, but ``better than the standard additive model'' and
``useful'' are different claims and only the first is supported there. Per population the
comparison is a coin flip ($72/136$, sign test $p=0.549$), so this is a magnitude loss on a
subset rather than uniform degradation --- which bounds the claim without rescuing it.

\textbf{The rank $r$ is global, not selected per fold.} $r{=}8$ was chosen once on held-out
singletons and held fixed across all five folds. A per-fold selector with a margin gate is
implemented but exercised only at reduced scale, where the gate declined to move off the
default on both folds it ran (candidates within $10^{-4}$ of each other), i.e.\ $r$ is not
identifiable at $26$--$29$ records per fold. We therefore make no claim about per-fold rank
behaviour; the full-scale sweep costs roughly $2400$--$2750$\,s against the $1890$\,s screen
and was not run.

\textbf{A finance ``singleton'' means one dominant channel, not one active channel.} No
$10$\,s window in four days of BTCUSDT has exactly one active channel at an any-trade
threshold, so stage 1 requires an activity floor to be identifiable. This is a weaker notion
of singleton than the organoid case, where single-agent populations are physically
realised.

\textbf{The interaction branch does not reach its oracle recovery ceiling.} On a synthetic
system with known ground truth, stage 2 improves the \emph{training} combinations it is
fitted on (combination loss $0.826 \to 0.241$) but not a leave-one-combination-out pair
($0.575 \to 1.060$). Two measured causes, neither of which is addressed by simplifying the
model: on the held-out pair the \emph{main} branch is already wrong (its cosine against the
additive-flow displacement is negative, $-0.318$, at $0.13$ of the additive scale), so the
failure begins before the interaction branch; and the kinematic composition defect is
$0.09$--$1.98\times$ the true interaction and near-orthogonal to it (maximum absolute cosine
$0.158$), so an $m_{2}$-gated branch fitted against a straight observed-space interpolant
necessarily absorbs a large non-interaction term. This is evidence about the interpolant.
No budget in the sweep of \S\ref{sec:budget} reaches the $0.018$ achievable floor either,
so the branch is demonstrably useful (\S\ref{sec:budget}) without being well-fitted.

\textbf{The headline ablation is budget-dependent, and we report the unfavourable number
alongside the favourable one.} At the $800$-step budget under which every arm in
Table~\ref{\tabonelabel} was compared, the interaction hierarchy is a null
($+0.015$ $[-0.024, +0.051]$). It becomes decisive only at $6400$ steps
($-0.175$ $[-0.275, -0.076]$, $8/8$ cells). A reader who cares only about the matched-budget
comparison should read the null as our result; we did not re-run the baselines at the larger
budget, so we cannot say the ordering against them would be preserved. The sweep is also
smaller than the main table ($8$ cells against $21$ folds, one benchmark instance,
$r$ fixed).

\textbf{The exposure axis is a distribution-shift test, not extrapolation.} See
\S\ref{sec:exp}; the oracle-transfer ratios are $1.799$--$2.802$ and the window-purged arm's
pre-state shift is three orders of magnitude above within-train.

\textbf{The population encoder hurts combinations} ($-0.059$ $[-0.113, -0.011]$, $p = 0.016$)
and is off by default. We inherited the mechanism from \citet{atanackovic2024mfm} and report
that it does not transfer to this setting.

\textbf{Single-fold, single-seed comparisons are underpowered.} Adjacent models on our
benchmark need $\sim\!270$--$450$ folds to separate at the observed effect size, against the
$6$ we ran. Where models are indistinguishable we say so rather than implying an order.

\textbf{Chart covariance is an operator-level guarantee plus one retraining test.} It is not
a general proof that training is chart-independent, and the retraining test says it is not:
the cross-chart discrepancy of \emph{trained} models correlates with chart curvature at
$+0.92$ (regression slope $2.18$; the chart-2 model is worse in $17$ of $18$ configurations).
At the strongest chart curvature the discrepancy ($0.0875$) reaches the seed-noise floor
($0.0875$), \ie training outcome is chart-dependent at the level of seed noise, and only
there. Both facts are ours to report: the operator is covariant to $10^{-16}$, the training
outcome is not exactly invariant, and we make no claim that it is.

\textbf{Scale.} All results are at $d \le 8$ and $k \le 6$, on CPU. The identifiability of a
state-dependent interaction term degrades with dimension, and we have not measured where.

\section{Propositions: proofs and full verification}
\label{app:props}

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{prop:defect}.} Let $g(t) = \psi(z + t\delta)$. Since $\psi$ is
$C^{2}$, Taylor with integral remainder at $t=0$ evaluated at $t=1$ gives
$\psi(z+\delta) = \psi(z) + D\psi(z)\delta + \int_{0}^{1}(1-t) D^{2}\psi(z+t\delta)[\delta,\delta]\,dt$.
Bounding the integrand by $M = \sup\|D^{2}\psi\|$ on the segment and using
$\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)dt = 1/2$ gives $\|R\| \le (M/2)\|\delta\|^{2}$; expanding
$D^{2}\psi$ about $t=0$ inside the integral isolates the leading coefficient
$\tfrac12 D^{2}\psi(z)[\delta,\delta]$. Only the symmetric part of $D^{2}\psi$ contributes,
being contracted against $\delta^{i}\delta^{j}$. For an affine $\psi$ the integrand vanishes
identically, so $R \equiv 0$ exactly and for arbitrarily large $\delta$.

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{prop:conn}.} Differentiating $\tilde X^{a} = J^{a}_{i}X^{i}$ with
respect to $z'^{b}$ and using $\partial_{b} = K^{j}_{b}\partial_{j}$ with $K = J^{-1}$ gives
$(D\tilde X[\tilde Y])^{a} = J^{a}_{i}(DX[Y])^{i} + D^{2}\psi^{a}[X,Y]$. Swapping $X$ and $Y$
yields the same second term, since $D^{2}\psi$ is symmetric in its lower indices; adding the
two copies gives $\tilde S = JS + 2D^{2}\psi[X,Y]$, and the factor $2$ is exactly the two
summands of $S$. For the connection, chart-independence of covariant differentiation,
$(\nabla_{\tilde Y}\tilde X)^{a} = J^{a}_{i}(\nabla_{Y}X)^{i}$, combined with the previous
identity, forces
$\tilde\Gamma^{a}[JX, JY] = J^{a}_{i}\Gamma^{i}[X,Y] - D^{2}\psi^{a}[X,Y]$. Adding the first
identity to twice the second cancels $D^{2}\psi$ identically. For $S + c\Gamma$ the residual
defect is $(2-c)D^{2}\psi$, so $c=2$ is forced and $c=-2$ carries defect $4D^{2}\psi$ ---
twice as large as no correction at all.

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{prop:cov}.} Because $\Dec' = \Dec\circ\psi^{-1}$ and
$z' = \psi(z)$, we have $\Dec'(z') = \Dec(z) = x$: the ambient point is the same in both
charts, so $W$ and $b$ are evaluated at the same argument and contribute no transformation
factors. The chain rule gives $J_{\Dec'} = J_{\Dec}K$, whence
$G' = K^{\top}(J_{\Dec}^{\top}WJ_{\Dec})K = K^{\top}GK$ and
$\alpha' = K^{\top}J_{\Dec}^{\top}b = K^{\top}\alpha$. Note $\alpha$ is the \emph{pullback of
a one-form}, which needs no inverse of the non-square $J_{\Dec}$; pushing a vector forward
would not be defined. Then
$v' = (K^{\top}GK)^{-1}K^{\top}\alpha = K^{-1}G^{-1}K^{-\top}K^{\top}\alpha = Jv$. The
construction is index-balanced: the $K^{-\top}K^{\top}$ pair annihilates and a single
$K^{-1} = J$ survives. Any non-tensorial addition to $G$ breaks that pairing and leaves a
residual factor of $J$.

\paragraph{A failure found and fixed, reported because it is a genuine trap.} A first pass
drew $\psi$ with an unconstrained nonlinearity, and at $2$ of $9$ seeds the covariance checks
failed catastrophically (Proposition~\ref{prop:conn} at $2.46$, Proposition~\ref{prop:cov} at
$6.30\times10^{-1}$) while the image-space residual $\psi(\psi^{-1}(z')) - z'$ stayed at
$3\times10^{-16}$. Cause: the drawn nonlinearity violated a contraction bound, $D\psi$ became
near-singular on the sample ($\sigma_{\min}$ down to $2.0\times10^{-3}$), $\psi$ was not
injective, and the Newton inverse converged to a \emph{different branch}. An image-space
check cannot detect this --- any branch satisfies it --- whereas the $Z$-space round trip
does ($\|\psi^{-1}(\psi(z)) - z\| = 8.2\times10^{-1}$). Both fixes are retained: the chart
rescales its nonlinear block at construction to enforce the contraction bound, and a
$Z$-space round trip is asserted at $10^{-13}$. The lesson generalises: every covariance
guarantee here is a statement about a single chart, so any experiment that reparameterises
data must verify injectivity on the data region \emph{in $Z$}, not merely in image space.
