30 method and architecture papers
JANUARY 2025 - 5 SEPTEMBER 2026
A curated review of 30 methodological and architectural papers first released on arXiv from January 2025 
onward, with a search cutoff of 5 September 2026. Selection favors distinct changes to learning objectives, 
transport paths, couplings, prediction targets, and model structure, including closely related discrete and 
stochastic-interpolant methods.
Search starting points
Discovery began with the foundational flow-matching and rectified-flow lines associated with Yaron Lipman, 
Ricky T. Q. Chen, and Qiang Liu. It then followed stochastic-interpolant work by Michael S. Albergo and Eric 
Vanden-Eijnden, and fast-generation work involving Kaiming He and Stefano Ermon. Author publication lists, 
related-work references, and targeted arXiv searches expanded the candidates; these names are search 
seeds, not a bibliometric ranking.
Chen: publications | Liu: research page
How to read the entries
Each entry separates the paper's claimed problem and proposed method from a retrospective design 
rationale. The three rationale points are an explanatory reconstruction of the method's motivation, not the 
authors' actual discovery process or a private reasoning transcript.

01 Mean Flows for One-step Generative Modeling
arxiv.org/abs/2505.13447 | First release: May 2025
Method text (v1) | Sections 4.1-4.2
Claimed problem. Standard flow matching learns instantaneous velocity, so generation requires 
numerical integration and can deteriorate when compressed to one step. The paper seeks a model 
trained directly for finite-interval transport without a pretrained teacher.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Make the prediction target describe displacement across an interval, since that is the quantity a 
one-step sampler needs.
2. Connect this average velocity to the instantaneous velocity using a differentiable identity, 
providing supervision without observing exact transport trajectories.
3. Condition the network on both interval endpoints and train the identity so the same model 
supports large or small sampling steps.
Proposed method. MeanFlow learns interval-averaged velocity using an identity relating it to 
instantaneous velocity and its total derivative, evaluated with a Jacobian-vector product. A stop-gradient
regression objective enables training from scratch and single-evaluation generation.
02 Improved Mean Flows: On the Challenges of Fastforward Generative Models
arxiv.org/abs/2512.02012 | First release: December 2025
Method text (v1) | Method sections on parameterization and guidance
Claimed problem. MeanFlow training couples average-velocity prediction to noisy conditional velocity 
targets, which can make the effective prediction target-dependent and difficult to optimize. Fast 
generation also needs flexible guidance rather than a single baked-in guidance scale.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Separate the quantity used to form the network prediction from the noisy sample-specific 
supervision.
2. Use a predicted marginal velocity in the derivative direction, then supervise the resulting 
composite velocity prediction with the accessible conditional target.
3. Expose guidance strength as an input so a trained fast generator can change the quality-diversity 
tradeoff at inference.
Proposed method. Improved Mean Flows replaces the target-dependent derivative direction with a 
predicted velocity, obtained from the average-velocity network or an auxiliary velocity head. It also 
conditions on guidance strength to support adjustable guided generation in very few evaluations.

03 How to build a consistency model: Learning flow maps via self-distillation
arxiv.org/abs/2505.18825 | First release: May 2025
Method text (v1) | Sections 2.2-2.3
Claimed problem. Flow-map and consistency models can accelerate sampling, but conventional 
approaches often require a separately trained velocity model or delicate direct-training objectives. The 
paper asks how to learn instantaneous dynamics and finite-time maps together.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Represent a finite-time map so its infinitesimal limit exposes an ordinary velocity field.
2. Use flow-matching supervision at coincident times to learn the local dynamics from data.
3. Constrain maps at separated times through differential or composition identities, allowing the 
developing model to supervise its own longer transitions.
Proposed method. The paper parameterizes a two-time flow map with an Euler-like residual form and 
combines diagonal flow matching with off-diagonal self-distillation. Lagrangian, Eulerian, and semigroupbased objectives provide alternative consistency constraints without requiring a separately pretrained 
teacher.
04 Align Your Flow: Scaling Continuous-Time Flow Map Distillation
arxiv.org/abs/2506.14603 | First release: June 2025
Method text (v1) | Sections 3.1-3.4
Claimed problem. The paper identifies error accumulation and unfavorable scaling with additional steps 
in the consistency formulations it analyzes. It seeks continuous-time distillation that remains useful 
across sampling budgets and scales to large image generators.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Predict transport between arbitrary noise levels instead of committing every prediction to the final
endpoint.
2. Require the predicted map's derivatives to agree with the teacher dynamics, yielding constraints 
that remain meaningful when transitions are composed.
3. Stabilize the time representation and derivative-based optimization, then include the desired 
guidance in the distilled behavior.
Proposed method. Align Your Flow introduces continuous-time flow-map distillation objectives derived 
from Eulerian and Lagrangian descriptions. Time embeddings, tangent warmup, and guidance distillation
make the approach practical at scale; the framework also permits adversarial refinement.

05 Flow Map Distillation Without Data
arxiv.org/abs/2511.19428 | First release: November 2025
Method text (v1) | Sections 3-4
Claimed problem. Data used for distillation may be unavailable or mismatched to a teacher that has 
been post-trained, while approximate teacher trajectories introduce another source of error. The goal is 
to distill a teacher's own distribution using only its prior and dynamics.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Use prior samples as the common starting distribution because the teacher's generative process is
defined from that prior.
2. Evaluate teacher consistency at states predicted by the student, avoiding dependence on an 
external training dataset.
3. Add a corrective noising construction to reduce errors that would otherwise compound along the 
learned transport.
Proposed method. The paper trains a prior-anchored flow map by matching its induced dynamics to the 
pretrained teacher at student-generated states. A noising-flow correction addresses accumulated 
transport errors, enabling data-free distillation while retaining access to the teacher.
06 Inductive Moment Matching
arxiv.org/abs/2503.07565 | First release: March 2025
Method text (PDF) | Sections 3.1-3.2
Claimed problem. Training effective few-step generators often depends on expensive teacher 
distillation or unstable, highly tuned consistency objectives. The paper seeks a distribution-level 
objective that learns flexible step sizes directly from data.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Require a transition to reproduce the correct intermediate distribution rather than a particular 
samplewise target.
2. Compare generated and reference distributions through kernel moments, which also supply 
interactions between generated samples.
3. Bootstrap longer transitions from already easier, shorter ones so a single model progressively 
learns coarse transport.
Proposed method. Inductive Moment Matching combines marginal-preserving interpolants with an 
inductive bootstrapping construction. A maximum mean discrepancy objective matches distributions 
across direct and bootstrapped transitions, supporting few-step generation from scratch without a 
pretrained teacher.

07 Improving Rectified Flow with Boundary Conditions
arxiv.org/abs/2506.15864 | First release: June 2025
Method text (v2) | Sections 3.1-3.3
Claimed problem. Under the paper's independent Gaussian-noise coupling and linear interpolation, 
endpoint velocities have known analytical forms that unconstrained networks need not satisfy. 
Boundary errors can become especially damaging when velocity is converted into a score near an 
endpoint.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Derive the endpoint behavior implied by the chosen coupling and noise distribution before fitting 
the network.
2. Encode those known values directly in the prediction formula.
3. Multiply the learned residual by time-dependent factors that vanish at the boundaries, leaving the
network to learn only the unconstrained interior behavior.
Proposed method. The paper introduces velocity parameterizations that impose analytical boundary 
conditions through fixed terms and gated neural residuals. Mask-based and subtraction-based 
constructions adapt existing architectures while improving endpoint consistency and the stability of 
derived quantities.
08 Understanding, Accelerating, and Improving MeanFlow Training
arxiv.org/abs/2511.19065 | First release: November 2025
Method text (v1) | Sections 4-5
Claimed problem. MeanFlow must learn accurate instantaneous velocity while also learning average 
velocity over longer intervals. The paper argues that these coupled tasks can interfere, slowing the 
development of the local dynamics needed for successful fast generation.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Treat local velocity learning as a prerequisite that deserves explicit attention during optimization.
2. Improve the sampling and weighting of local supervision so useful instantaneous dynamics 
develop earlier.
3. Gradually broaden emphasis from short intervals to longer ones as those dynamics become 
reliable.
Proposed method. The proposed training strategy accelerates instantaneous-velocity learning through 
timestep sampling and weighting, then progressively adjusts average-velocity loss emphasis. This 
changes the optimization schedule of MeanFlow rather than replacing its underlying average-velocity 
formulation.

09 Asymmetric Flow Models
arxiv.org/abs/2605.12964 | First release: May 2026
Method text (v1) | Core parameterization and latent-to-pixel construction
Claimed problem. Pixel-space transformers face a costly high-dimensional noise-prediction burden, 
while pure clean-data prediction can become poorly conditioned at low noise. The paper seeks a 
parameterization between full velocity prediction and pure data prediction.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Separate the information needed to describe clean structure from the information needed to 
represent noise.
2. Preserve full-rank data prediction while restricting the learned noise component to a lowerdimensional subspace.
3. Recover the missing velocity contribution analytically from the current state, preserving a usable 
flow field.
Proposed method. Asymmetric Flow Models combine full-rank data prediction with low-rank noise 
prediction and analytically reconstruct the complete velocity. The rank controls a continuum between 
data and velocity prediction, and the formulation supports transferring latent-space models into pixelspace generation.
10 Variational Rectified Flow Matching
arxiv.org/abs/2502.09616 | First release: February 2025
Method text (v1) | Sections 3.1-3.3
Claimed problem. Multiple conditional trajectories can assign different velocities to the same 
intermediate state, so squared-error regression averages incompatible directions and can produce 
curved marginal paths. This concerns ambiguity in the velocity targets, not an inability of flow matching 
to represent multimodal data.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Represent alternative velocity directions explicitly instead of forcing them into one conditional 
mean.
2. Introduce a latent variable that distinguishes plausible transport behaviors.
3. Learn the latent assignment variationally while regularizing it toward a sampling-time prior.
Proposed method. Variational Rectified Flow Matching uses a latent-conditioned velocity model and a 
variational encoder over transport examples. An evidence lower bound combines velocity regression 
with a KL regularizer, allowing the learned transport to represent conditional velocity ambiguity.

11 Beyond Optimal Transport: Model-Aligned Coupling for Flow Matching
arxiv.org/abs/2505.23346 | First release: May 2025
Method text (v1) | Section 3
Claimed problem. A coupling that is attractive under geometric transport cost may still be difficult for 
the current neural model to learn. The paper argues that training efficiency also depends on how well 
the chosen pairs align with the model's present predictive ability.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Include model learnability in the pairing criterion instead of measuring only endpoint distance.
2. Score candidate noise-data pairs with the current velocity-prediction loss.
3. Select the easier subset repeatedly as training evolves, creating an inexpensive adaptive pairing 
mechanism.
Proposed method. Model-Aligned Coupling uses model-dependent prediction error to choose training 
pairs. Its practical implementation retains a low-loss fraction of candidate pairs through top-k selection, 
avoiding an expensive full assignment while adapting the training distribution to the model.
12 COT-FM: Cluster-wise Optimal Transport Flow Matching
arxiv.org/abs/2603.13395 | First release: March 2026
Method text (v1) | Sections 3.1-3.2
Claimed problem. Random and minibatch transport pairings can poorly approximate the global 
structure of the data distribution, leaving difficult or curved transport paths. Computing an accurate 
global coupling directly is expensive.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Divide the data into coherent clusters so transport can be handled through smaller, structured 
subproblems.
2. Use an existing flow in reverse to estimate which source regions correspond to each cluster.
3. Solve local transport pairings within those regions and refine the model using the resulting better 
organized paths.
Proposed method. COT-FM combines data clustering, cluster-specific source estimation from a 
pretrained reverse flow, and local optimal-transport pairings. Alternating coupling construction and flow
refinement improves transport structure without requiring a new generator architecture.

13 Improving Progressive Generation with Decomposable Flow Matching
arxiv.org/abs/2506.19839 | First release: June 2025
Method text (v1) | Sections 3.2-3.4
Claimed problem. Progressive coarse-to-fine generators often need specialized stage transitions and 
samplers tied to a particular decomposition. The paper seeks a general flow-matching formulation that 
supports staged generation without rebuilding the generative process for each design.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Express the sample as components, such as coarse structure and successive detail bands.
2. Give each component its own progress variable so their generation schedules can differ.
3. Train a shared conditional model over these component states, then choose a staged schedule 
using ordinary flow integration.
Proposed method. Decomposable Flow Matching assigns separate time coordinates and velocity targets
to decomposed sample components. Its shared transformer uses component-aware input and time 
representations, enabling progressive generation, including a Laplacian-pyramid construction, within a 
unified flow framework.
14 Lagrangian Flow Matching: A Least-Action Framework for Principled Path Design
arxiv.org/abs/2605.15419 | First release: May 2026
Method text (v1) | Least-action formulation and conditional training construction
Claimed problem. Standard affine paths and kinetic optimal transport cover only a limited range of 
desirable transport behaviors. The paper asks how to design richer paths from an explicit principle 
rather than choosing interpolation formulas heuristically.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Specify preferred motion through an action that can penalize velocity and encode positiondependent behavior.
2. Use the corresponding least-action trajectories and endpoint transport costs to connect 
conditional paths with a distribution-level problem.
3. For tractable action choices, regress their conditional velocities using the flow-matching 
machinery.
Proposed method. Lagrangian Flow Matching formulates path learning through least action and a staticdynamic optimal-transport relationship. Appropriate Lagrangians recover straight kinetic paths or yield 
curved alternatives, such as harmonic-oscillator paths, with conditional regression objectives for 
tractable cases.

15 Generalized Wasserstein Flow Matching: Transport Plans, Everywhere, All at Once
arxiv.org/abs/2605.08424 | First release: May 2026
Method text (v1) | Sections 2.3 and 3
Claimed problem. When each example is itself a probability measure or particle set, ordinary Euclidean 
interpolation misses the geometry within and between examples. Exact nested optimal transport can be
too expensive for practical generative training.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Treat the dataset as a distribution over distributions, making both transport levels explicit.
2. Couple example measures at the outer level and their constituent particles at the inner level.
3. Use tractable Wasserstein approximations and a permutation-equivariant velocity network to 
preserve this structure computationally.
Proposed method. The framework combines outer and inner transport plans to construct flow-matching
targets on distributions of measures. Sliced and linearized Wasserstein approximations reduce cost, 
while equivariant particle-based models represent the resulting distribution-valued dynamics.
16 Bures-Wasserstein Flow Matching for Graph Generation
arxiv.org/abs/2506.14020 | First release: June 2025
Method text (v1) | Graph representation, Bures-Wasserstein paths, and flow training
Claimed problem. Independent interpolation of graph nodes and edges can overlook their joint 
structural dependencies. The paper seeks graph-generation paths that reflect relationships encoded by 
the whole graph.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Represent graph structure through an associated Markov random field, making dependencies part
of the transported object.
2. Choose interpolation from a Wasserstein geometry compatible with that representation.
3. Translate the structured interpolation into trainable node-and-edge dynamics instead of treating 
the components independently.
Proposed method. The paper builds graph flow matching around a Markov-random-field representation
and Bures-Wasserstein interpolation. The resulting structured paths couple node and edge evolution, 
with formulations that incorporate continuous and discrete flow-matching components.

17 Energy Guided Geometric Flow Matching
arxiv.org/abs/2509.25230 | First release: September 2025
Method text (v1) | Sections 4.1-4.3
Claimed problem. Straight Euclidean paths can leave a curved data manifold, but density-based 
geometric metrics may overfavor heavily sampled regions. The paper seeks geometry-aware paths that 
distinguish manifold support from uneven sampling density.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Learn an energy representation of the data support while identifying density imbalance as a 
separate source of bias.
2. Reweight the energy-learning process so dense regions do not dominate the inferred geometry.
3. Construct a metric from the corrected energy and train flow matching along its low-cost paths.
Proposed method. Energy Guided Geometric Flow Matching uses iterative density reweighting and 
annealing to learn an energy-based metric, then fits geometric paths and their flow velocities. The 
guiding energy describes learned data geometry rather than necessarily representing a physical energy 
or downstream reward.
18 Space Group Conditional Flow Matching
arxiv.org/abs/2509.23822 | First release: September 2025
Method text (v1) | Sections 3.1-3.3
Claimed problem. Conditioning a crystal generator on a space-group label alone does not guarantee 
that generated structures obey the requested symmetry. The paper seeks to preserve specified 
crystallographic symmetry throughout generation.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Require the initial distribution to satisfy the desired symmetry, not merely receive its label.
2. Require the velocity field to commute with the relevant symmetry operations.
3. Implement both conditions efficiently so every evolution step preserves the selected structural 
constraints.
Proposed method. The approach constructs a space-group-conditional prior using Wyckoff positions and
learns a space-group-equivariant velocity field. An optimized group-averaging procedure symmetrizes 
the model, preserving the initial symmetry along the generated flow.

19 Transition Matching: Scalable and Flexible Generative Modeling
arxiv.org/abs/2506.23589 | First release: June 2025
Method text (v1) | Sections 2-3
Claimed problem. Restrictive transition families, such as deterministic updates or simple noise kernels, 
limit the expressiveness available at each generative step. The paper seeks a framework that can learn 
richer finite-step transition distributions.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Make the transition kernel itself the learned object, allowing each step to express multiple 
plausible outcomes.
2. Obtain transition supervision from a chosen process connecting noise and data.
3. Separate expensive representation computation from a smaller generative transition head so 
expressive sampling need not repeatedly invoke the full backbone.
Proposed method. Transition Matching trains parameterized transition kernels to match those of a 
supervising process. Its diffusion-style and autoregressive instances use a large backbone with a small 
flow-based head; in the diffusion-style instance, multiple head evaluations can reuse backbone features.
20 A Few-Step Generative Model on Cumulative Flow Maps
arxiv.org/abs/2605.03623 | First release: May 2026
Method text (v1) | Sections 3.2-3.4
Claimed problem. Average-velocity formulations for fast generation do not directly provide a unified 
treatment of other prediction parameterizations and generative update rules. Moreover, a valid 
cumulative-field training target cannot simply be obtained by averaging conditional cumulative fields.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Describe each model's instantaneous update with an abstract transition function that separates 
the update rule from its predicted field.
2. Define a cumulative parameterization whose transition function represents transport over a finite 
interval.
3. Relate that field to instantaneous supervision and its derivatives, replacing the unavailable direct 
cumulative target with a trainable identity.
Proposed method. The paper generalizes finite-interval flow maps through cumulative parameterization
fields compatible with multiple update formulations. Derivative identities yield objectives for learning 
these fields, enabling one- or few-step sampling and reuse of pretrained instantaneous models.

21 Edit Flows: Flow Matching with Edit Operations
arxiv.org/abs/2506.09018 | First release: June 2025
Method text (v1) | Sections 3.1-3.2
Claimed problem. Fixed-length discrete flow models cannot naturally insert or delete tokens, while 
autoregressive generation makes arbitrary-position revision awkward. The paper seeks a generative 
process that changes both sequence content and length.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Use a state space containing sequences of different lengths.
2. Describe local evolution with insertion, deletion, and substitution events rather than only fixedposition token changes.
3. Construct an auxiliary process whose tractable conditional supervision can be marginalized to 
train the desired edit dynamics.
Proposed method. Edit Flows defines a continuous-time Markov process over variable-length sequences
with learned edit rates and token distributions. An augmented-process construction and flow-matchingstyle objectives make these insertion, deletion, and substitution dynamics trainable.
22 OneFlow: Concurrent Mixed-Modal and Interleaved Generation with Edit Flows
arxiv.org/abs/2510.03506 | First release: October 2025
Method text (v3) | Joint model, time parameterization, and sampling design
Claimed problem. Autoregressive multimodal systems impose a generation order, while fixed-layout 
diffusion systems constrain text length and interleaving. The paper seeks concurrent generation of 
variable-length text and continuous image content.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Give text length changes a discrete insertion process and image content a continuous flow.
2. Couple their predictions through one multimodal backbone so either modality can inform the 
other's development.
3. Coordinate modality-specific progress variables and sampling stages to support interleaved 
generation and refinement.
Proposed method. OneFlow combines insertion-based Edit Flows for text with continuous flow 
matching for images in a shared multimodal model. Image-specific time conditioning and coordinated 
hierarchical sampling support concurrent mixed-modal and interleaved outputs.

Claimed problem. Molecular generators with a fixed atom count cannot naturally morph a structured

RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Combine continuous coordinate motion with discrete events that change the molecular graph's

2. Align unequal-size endpoint molecules through transport that permits unmatched mass,

3. Use the aligned examples to supervise both coordinate velocities and molecular edit rates.

deletion, and substitution jump dynamics. Unbalanced optimal transport supplies symmetry-aware

24 Multitask Learning with Stochastic Interpolants
arxiv.org/abs/2508.04605 | First release: August 2025
Method text (v1) | Sections 2-3
Claimed problem. A stochastic interpolant controlled by a single scalar clock commits training to a 
narrow family of paths, limiting reuse across generation and inverse problems. The paper seeks a single 
learned representation that supports multiple task-dependent evolutions.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Replace the scalar interpolation schedule with operators that can control different coordinates or

2. Learn conditional endpoint statistics across this richer family of interpolants.

Proposed method. The paper introduces operator-valued stochastic interpolants and trains associated 
conditional drift components. Different operator trajectories then realize tasks such as generation, 
denoising, and inpainting without training a separate model for each task.

25 Physics vs Distributions: Pareto Optimal Flow Matching with Physics Constraints
arxiv.org/abs/2506.08604 | First release: June 2025
Method text (v4) | Physics-loss formulation, temporal unrolling, and Appendix F
Claimed problem. Physics residuals and distribution-matching losses can produce conflicting 
optimization directions. Applying nonlinear physical constraints to an intermediate denoised mean can 
also misrepresent the constraint violation of generated samples.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Evaluate physical constraints on a better approximation to an actual generated endpoint.
2. Use short differentiable temporal unrolling during training to obtain that endpoint approximation.
3. Coordinate the physical and statistical loss gradients so improving one objective does not 
needlessly oppose the other.
Proposed method. The framework combines flow-matching training with physical residual losses 
evaluated after temporal unrolling. It adopts conflict-free multiobjective gradient updates to balance 
physical consistency and distributional fidelity, moving constraint enforcement into training rather than 
adding it to every inference step.
26 Multimarginal flow matching with optimal transport potentials
arxiv.org/abs/2606.05327 | First release: June 2026
Method text (v2) | Sections 3.2-4
Claimed problem. Endpoint-only flow matching discards intermediate population observations, while 
separately connecting adjacent snapshots can create abrupt changes in velocity. The paper seeks a 
coherent transport model that incorporates multiple observed distributions.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Treat intermediate distributions as constraints on one global evolution rather than independent 
endpoint pairs.
2. Add time-localized distributional potentials to a transport action so the dynamics respond

Proposed method. The paper combines a dynamic optimal-transport objective with potentials that 
penalize mismatch to intermediate marginals. Conditional trajectory regression and a fixed-point 
consistency construction provide a flow-matching approach to learning the resulting transport.

27 GLASS Flows: Transition Sampling for Alignment of Flow and Diffusion Models
arxiv.org/abs/2509.25170 | First release: September 2025
Method text (v1) | Section 4
Claimed problem. Reward-based branching methods need stochastic transitions, but pretrained flow 
ODEs are deterministic and adding SDE simulation can be costly. The paper seeks flexible conditional 
transition sampling from existing flow or diffusion models.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Regard a stochastic transition as its own conditional generative problem.
2. Design a Gaussian conditional construction whose observations reduce to a sufficient statistic 
usable by the pretrained denoiser.
3. Inject fresh randomness through an inner conditional flow, supplying branching transitions 
without retraining the base model.
Proposed method. GLASS constructs tunable stochastic transitions using Gaussian conditional structure 
and sufficient-statistic transformations. An inner flow reuses the pretrained model to sample those 
transitions, enabling reward-guided branching and sequential Monte Carlo alignment without finetuning.
28 Tilt Matching for Scalable Sampling and Fine-Tuning

Claimed problem. Sampling from unnormalized reward-tilted distributions or adapting a generator may 
lack target examples and tractable likelihoods. The paper seeks updates that use scalar rewards without

RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Introduce the desired reward tilt gradually through a continuous family of target distributions.

Proposed method. Tilt Matching derives velocity evolution from a conditional covariance involving 
reward and interpolant dynamics. Its iterative regression procedure uses scalar reward evaluations to 
transport toward tilted targets, supporting both unnormalized sampling and generator fine-tuning.

29 Discrete Tilt Matching
arxiv.org/abs/2604.18739 | First release: April 2026
Method text (v1) | Section 3
Claimed problem. Sequence likelihoods in masked generative language models require accounting for 
many unmasking orders, making conventional reward-based updates difficult or noisy. The paper seeks 
a tractable local objective for reward adaptation.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Work with local unmasking posteriors instead of the intractable full sequence likelihood.
2. Tilt these local conditional distributions with reward information and fit them through weighted 
cross-entropy.
3. Apply the tilt incrementally and introduce control variates that reduce variance while preserving 
the desired minimizer.
Proposed method. Discrete Tilt Matching adapts masked discrete generators through reward-weighted 
local posterior matching. Annealed updates and minimizer-preserving control variates make the 
weighted cross-entropy objective practical; this is a discrete generative-flow relative rather than 
ordinary continuous-state flow matching.
30 LEAPS: A discrete neural sampler via locally equivariant networks
arxiv.org/abs/2502.10843 | First release: February 2025
Method text (v2) | Training and importance sampling; Sections 8-9
Claimed problem. Sampling unnormalized discrete distributions is difficult when Markov chains mix 
slowly and target training samples are unavailable. Generic neural transition-rate models also make the 
correction terms needed for reliable importance weighting expensive.
RETROSPECTIVE DESIGN RATIONALE
1. Define an annealed continuous-time jump process and learn its generator by enforcing the 
probability-evolution equation.
2. Build local equivariance into the rate representation so forward and reverse neighbor 
contributions can be evaluated efficiently.
3. Use tractable importance-weight updates to account for remaining errors in the learned dynamics.
Proposed method. LEAPS combines physics-informed training of a discrete Markov generator with 
locally equivariant MLP, attention, and convolutional architectures. The resulting rate structure enables 
efficient proactive importance sampling and integration with annealing or particle-based samplers for 
data-free discrete sampling.
